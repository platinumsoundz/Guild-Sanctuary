# Platform comparison and legal notes

Last updated: 2026-10-24

This document compares The Sanctuary with social, community, creator, and wellbeing platforms that are useful reference points. It records the legal and moderation paths those platforms publish, what The Sanctuary can learn from them, and which gaps still block a public launch with paid tokens.

**This is product and policy research, not legal advice.** The platform policies below can change. The Sanctuary still needs qualified review for its operator details, jurisdiction, consumer terms, privacy notice, age assurance, moderation process, and refund/chargeback handling.

## 1. Why these platforms

The Sanctuary is not a generic feed. It is a small community for reflection, growth, care, and spiritual or philosophical connection. The closest comparisons are therefore a mix of:

- **Large social platforms** for age, safety, reporting, and enforcement at scale: Meta, TikTok, Reddit, Discord.
- **Creator and paid-community platforms** for digital purchases, subscriptions, and creator/community boundaries: Patreon, Substack.
- **Real-world community platforms** for events and local trust: Meetup.
- **Wellbeing and contemplative platforms** for teacher credibility, crisis signposting, and safer spiritual language: Insight Timer, 7 Cups.

No single platform is a perfect model. The useful move is to combine their strongest patterns without copying their scale assumptions.

## 3. What each platform actually publishes

### Meta

Meta’s Terms of Service bar anyone under 13 and require users to meet the legal age in their country. Meta has been moving toward stronger teen defaults and age-assurance processes, including teen accounts and age estimation. Its Community Standards are organized by harm category and are backed by reporting, appeals, and periodic enforcement reports.

**What to take:** A public standards page organized by harm, not by vague “be nice” language. A separate teen experience is safer than treating every account the same. Enforcement reporting builds trust, even at a small scale.

**What not to copy:** The assumption that scale justifies opaque enforcement. The Sanctuary should keep decisions explainable and appealable.

### TikTok

TikTok requires users to be at least 13 to create an account, with local variations. Its Virtual Items Policy requires users to be at least 18 (or the age of majority, whichever is older) to buy, use, or collect certain virtual items such as Coins and Diamonds. TikTok’s Community Guidelines include age-restricted content, youth-safety rules, and reporting paths.

**What to take:** The clean split between “community age” and “purchase age.” The Sanctuary already plans 13+ community and 18+ token purchases; TikTok shows why the purchase rule needs its own gate, disclosure, and terms, not just a line in the general terms.

**What not to copy:** A virtual currency that is deeply entangled with creator gifting and rankings. The Sanctuary’s current scope — permanent cosmetics, no cash-out, no transfers — is deliberately simpler and safer.

### Discord

Discord requires users to be at least 13, with higher minimums in some countries. It has been rolling out a teen-by-default experience, age-assurance work, and age-restricted spaces. Its Community Guidelines cover harassment, hate, sexual content, self-harm, and child safety. Its paid-services terms say purchases are generally non-refundable except where law requires otherwise; Nitro refunds have a short 5-day window, and profile cosmetics/in-app purchases are generally not refundable.

**What to take:** Space-level moderation is powerful. Discord lets community owners set rules, age-gate channels, and moderate locally while the platform keeps a safety floor. The Sanctuary could later support small circles or hosted spaces with named stewards, provided the platform retains ultimate safety authority.

**What not to copy:** A confusing mix of platform, server, and seller rules. The Sanctuary should state clearly who can remove content, who can suspend an account, and where an appeal goes.

### Reddit

Reddit’s User Agreement requires users to be at least 13 and old enough to form a binding contract in their jurisdiction; mature content is 18+. Reddit combines a site-wide Content Policy with subreddit-specific rules. Moderation and appeals are handled at both levels, and enforcement depends on severity and history.

**What to take:** A two-layer model: a site-wide minimum standard plus community-specific norms. The Sanctuary already has a single community, but it can still distinguish platform-wide safety rules from topic-specific etiquette (for example, grief, meditation, or philosophical debate).

**What not to copy:** Fragmented accountability. A small platform should not make users guess which moderator or rule applied.

### Patreon

Patreon is not directed to children under 13. Users must be at least 18 or have parent/guardian permission to have a creator page or purchase an offering; Adult/18+ creators and subscribers have additional verification. Patreon’s terms say it generally does not provide refunds, though it may make discretionary exceptions, and creators can issue refunds for a limited window.

**What to take:** Separate the age to use the platform from the age to earn or pay. Give the person running a paid space clear refund and content-boundary tools. Make it obvious that a purchase is for a specific offering, not for a vague promise of future access.

**What not to copy:** A purely discretionary refund stance. The Sanctuary’s approved policy is more user-protective: claw back unspent tokens and manually review spent-token cases.

### Substack

Substack’s Terms of Use state that users must be at least 16. Its Content Guidelines prohibit porn, sexual exploitation, harmful or illegal activity, and certain other categories, while leaving much day-to-day moderation to publication owners.

**What to take:** Long-form, creator-led spaces can work with a small central team if the content guidelines and owner responsibilities are explicit. The Sanctuary could support reflective essays, practice journals, or hosted circles without becoming a full social network.

**What not to copy:** A hands-off moderation posture that can leave marginalized users exposed. A wellbeing community needs a more active safety floor.

### Meetup

Meetup requires members and organizers to be at least 18 and requires groups and events to be targeted to adults. It publishes group and event policies covering harmful misinformation, sexual exploitation, and other categories.

**What to take:** Real-world events need host accountability, clear cancellation and safety expectations, and a way to remove a host who behaves badly. If The Sanctuary adds meetups, it should start with small, moderated, local gatherings and a named host.

**What not to copy:** An adult-only default if The Sanctuary wants a 13+ online community. The online and offline age rules may need to differ.

### Insight Timer

Insight Timer’s terms restrict children under 13 and require additional care for under-16 data. Its teacher guidelines prohibit medical claims, substance promotion, and content led by children under 16; live events are 18+. Its community guidelines describe consequences for violations.

**What to take:** Teacher/guide credibility matters in a contemplative community. The Sanctuary should distinguish a member sharing personal experience from a “teacher” making health claims or selling authority. It should also keep a calm, non-hype tone in paid features.

**What not to copy:** A large teacher marketplace. The Sanctuary’s current curated, small catalog is a better fit for trust.

### 7 Cups

7 Cups provides anonymous emotional support for people aged 13 and up, with teen-specific safety protocols, listener training, blocking, reporting, and crisis escalation. It is explicit that it is not a replacement for emergency services.

**What to take:** Crisis signposting, listener/mod training, teen-specific safeguards, and easy blocking/reporting are not optional in a wellbeing-adjacent community. The Sanctuary should publish a crisis-resource line and train moderators on self-harm, abuse, and exploitation reports.

**What not to copy:** The implication that peer support is therapy. The Sanctuary should be clear about what it is and is not.

## 4. Legal baseline for a UK-facing launch

The project prices in GBP and appears UK-facing. The following are the main legal areas to review with counsel. They are not a substitute for advice.

### 4.1 Age and children’s data

- **Community age:** The current decision is 13+. This matches the common platform floor and the UK’s approach to children’s data, but it triggers additional duties because the service is likely to be accessed by children.
- **Purchase age:** Token purchases are 18+. This should be a separate gate with its own disclosure, not a checkbox buried in general terms.
- **Age assurance:** Self-declared date of birth is weak. The UK Online Safety Act and Ofcom’s children’s safety duties push services likely to be accessed by children toward age assurance for certain features. A small community may not need highly effective age assurance for all content, but it should document its risk assessment and use a proportionate gate for paid features.
- **Children’s code:** The UK Age Appropriate Design Code (Children’s Code) applies to services likely to be accessed by children. It affects default privacy, data minimisation, profiling, nudge techniques, and parental controls.
- **Teen safety:** Consider a teen default with stricter discovery, DM, and spending settings, even if the product is 13+.

### 4.2 Consumer contracts and digital content

- **14-day cancellation:** The Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013 give consumers a 14-day cancellation right for distance contracts, including digital content not supplied on a tangible medium.
- **Digital content exception:** The right can be lost once supply begins only if the consumer gave **prior express consent** to immediate supply and **acknowledged** that the right to cancel would be lost. The trader must also provide the required pre-contract information.
- **Practical implication:** If tokens are credited immediately, the checkout flow should record consent and acknowledgment, or the user may retain a cancellation right. This must be reflected in the Terms and checkout UI before live sales.
- **Consumer Rights Act 2015:** Digital content must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose, and as described. If it is not, the consumer may have remedies. Do not draft terms that try to remove statutory rights.
- **Refunds and chargebacks:** The approved policy — claw back unspent tokens; flag spent-token cases for manual review — is reasonable, but it must be written into the Terms and implemented in the ledger. A chargeback is not the same as a voluntary refund; the payment processor’s dispute process and evidence requirements also apply.

### 4.3 Privacy and data protection

- **UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018:** Identify the controller, lawful bases, retention periods, subprocessors, and international transfer mechanism. The app uses Supabase, Stripe, and a hosting provider; AdSense is deferred.
- **Data subject rights:** Access, correction, deletion, restriction, objection, and portability. The 7-day deletion grace period needs a clear explanation of what is hidden, what is retained, and what is purged.
- **Special category data:** Spiritual or philosophical beliefs can be special category data. The app currently lets users display “belief” and “star sign” fields. If those are published, the privacy notice needs a clear lawful basis and explicit consent where required. Consider making belief fields private by default.
- **Retention:** Posts, messages, media, wallet history, moderation records, and backups need separate retention rules. Do not promise immediate erasure from backups if that is not true.
- **Processors:** Supabase, Stripe, and the host are processors or independent controllers depending on the service. The privacy notice should name categories and link to their policies.

### 4.4 Online safety and moderation

- **Online Safety Act 2023:** User-to-user services have duties around illegal content risk assessments, illegal content, content reporting, complaints, and record-keeping. Services likely to be accessed by children have additional children’s safety duties. Ofcom’s timelines have been moving; the project should not assume a small community is out of scope.
- **CSEA reporting:** From 7 April 2026, regulated providers must report detected and unreported child sexual abuse material to the National Crime Agency. The Sanctuary needs a documented escalation path, not an ad hoc moderator decision.
- **Reporting and complaints:** Publish how to report, what happens next, and how to appeal. Keep an audit log. The current moderation dashboard is a start but does not yet cover the full admin workflow.
- **Transparency:** Even a small platform can publish a short annual safety report: reports received, actions taken, appeals, and response times. This builds trust and creates an internal compliance habit.

### 4.5 Payments and advertising

- **Stripe:** Use a server-created Checkout Session tied to the authenticated account. Never trust client-supplied prices or user IDs. Keep webhook signature verification and idempotency.
- **18+ gate:** Token purchases must be adult-only. This is both a platform-policy choice and a consumer-protection safeguard.
- **Pre-contract information:** Show the token amount, price, currency, immediate-supply consent, cancellation-loss acknowledgment, and refund/chargeback policy before payment.
- **Advertising:** AdSense is deferred until a custom domain exists. If ads return, the Google EU user consent policy and a certified CMP may apply in the UK/EEA. Do not serve ads to children without appropriate safeguards.

## 5. What The Sanctuary should adopt next

### Must-have before public launch with free tokens

1. **Publish the Community Guidelines page.** Done in this change: `src/app/guidelines/page.tsx`.
2. **Publish a clearly marked Terms draft.** Done in this change: `src/app/terms/page.tsx`, with `noindex` and a “not in force” banner.
3. **Add a crisis-resource line** to the guidelines and support surfaces. Done in the guidelines page; add it to the support panel next.
4. **Make belief and star-sign fields private by default.** Review the profile privacy defaults and the public profile view.
5. **Add a report → triage → action → appeal → audit path.** The moderation dashboard exists; the appeal/notes/role-management pieces do not.
6. **Document the moderation escalation path for self-harm, abuse, exploitation, and CSEA.** This is a policy and training task, not only code.

### Must-have before live token sales

1. **18+ purchase gate** with an explicit age confirmation and server-side record.
2. **Pre-contract token disclosure** and recorded immediate-supply consent / cancellation-loss acknowledgment.
3. **Refund/chargeback clawback** with unspent-token reversal and manual-review flagging for spent tokens.
4. **Admin dashboard** covering reports, suspend/ban, wallet/token history, audit log, appeals/notes, and role management.
5. **Approved Terms and Privacy Notice** with a durable version/timestamp acceptance record.
6. **Staging validation** of RLS, Edge Functions, webhook idempotency, wrong-mode/wrong-price rejection, and duplicate delivery.

### Should-have after launch

1. **Teen defaults:** stricter discovery, DM, and spending settings for 13–17.
2. **Space-level stewardship:** small circles or hosted spaces with named stewards and platform safety authority.
3. **Teacher/guide labels:** distinguish personal experience from professional or medical authority.
4. **Transparency report:** a short annual summary of reports, actions, appeals, and response times.
5. **Data export and deletion status:** a visible “what will be deleted / what will be retained” explanation during the 7-day grace period.
6. **Chat emoji packs:** the next expression feature, with the same server-owned catalog and entitlement model as profile cosmetics.

## 6. Inspiration for the spiritual / mind-opening concept

The comparison suggests a product that is calmer and more intentional than a general social network:

- **From Discord:** small, interest-based spaces with clear local norms and age-gated rooms.
- **From Reddit:** a site-wide safety floor plus topic-specific etiquette for grief, meditation, philosophy, and debate.
- **From Meetup:** real-world gatherings with named hosts, clear cancellation terms, and safety expectations.
- **From Insight Timer:** credibility signals for guides, no medical claims, no substance promotion, and a calm tone.
- **From 7 Cups:** crisis signposting, listener/mod training, blocking, reporting, and teen safeguards.
- **From Patreon/Substack:** long-form reflection and paid community support without turning every interaction into a transaction.
- **From TikTok:** short-form discovery can bring people in, but it should not drive the community’s values.

A useful product principle: **connection first, content second, commerce last.** Tokens should buy expression and support, not reach, status, or another person’s attention. The current scope — permanent cosmetics, no transfers, no cash-out, no gifting — is aligned with that principle. Keep it until the safety and refund foundations are real.

## 9. Questions for the product owner

1. Which legal entity and jurisdiction will operate The Sanctuary?
2. What support email and postal address will appear in the Terms and Privacy Notice?
3. Should the community remain 13+, or should it be 16+ or 18+ to reduce safeguarding and data-protection burden?
4. Should “belief” and “star sign” be private by default?
5. What is the exact refund window and evidence process for a chargeback?
6. Who reviews spent-token refund cases, and what is the target response time?
7. Should the 7-day deletion grace period also preserve moderation records and financial ledger entries?
8. Which moderation actions require a second reviewer?
9. Should teen accounts have direct messages disabled by default?
10. When will a custom domain be available for AdSense and email deliverability?
